# Editor, Writer, and Researcher Roles

Characteristics: Their day-to-day work involves summarizing articles, creating content outlines, organizing information, and turning it into materials.

Examples:
- Editor, at a publishing company or web media company
- Researcher
- Technical Writer

-----------------------

# Key Tasks

---

#1 Article Summarization: Information Gathering and Input Organization

Editors, writers, and researchers regularly summarize and organize information in order to read through large volumes of material and restructure it into a form that is easy for readers to understand.

## Specific Work Activities

- Read news articles, specialized books, academic papers, interview transcripts, and other materials; extract only the parts needed for articles or manuscripts; and summarize them into one to two pages.
- Research articles from competing media outlets and similar themes, and organize key points while comparing angles, structures, and arguments.
- Interpret research data and statistical information, and convert them into concise summaries that readers can easily understand while visualizing them in graphs.
- Read 100- to 300-page documents, extract only the key points needed for the article structure, and summarize them into an outline memo.
- Organize information into the structure of “Conclusion → Evidence → Value for the Reader,” leaving only the points directly linked to the direction of the article.

---

#2 Creating Instruction Documents: Design and Guidance for Production Management

Editors, writers, and researchers create instruction documents and outlines for stakeholders in order to ensure smooth article production and research work.

## Specific Work Activities

- Organize the article’s theme, objective, and target readership, and clarify them in text and diagrams as a proposal.
- Organize information on interview subjects and create interview request documents, including the purpose, draft questions, and required materials.
- Create article outlines, including proposed titles, headings, and section structures, and prepare them in a form that can be shared in editorial meetings.
- Create writing guidelines for writers, including tone and manner, writing style, citation rules, and prohibited items.
- Break down the production schedule and list deadlines, owners, and request details as a WBS, or Work Breakdown Structure.
- Organize the roles of photographers, illustrators, and designers, and create production instructions that clearly define required materials and delivery formats.
- Organize proofreading and fact-checking perspectives, and describe them as a checklist in the format of “who checks what by when.”

---

#3 Creating Presentation Materials: Proposals and Explanations for Editorial Meetings

Editors, writers, and researchers create presentation materials for editorial meetings and clients in order to get proposals approved.

## Specific Work Activities

- Build a logical storyline based on the proposal background, reader needs, and competitive landscape: background → issue → proposal intent → outline → expected impact.
- Create slides for editors-in-chief and clients that are conclusion-first, with one message per slide.
- Visualize reader data and access analytics in graphs, and clearly state the “So What,” or what matters most, on each slide.
- Translate the proposal into article structure, required materials, production setup, and schedule, organizing it in a form that makes approval easier.
- Adjust expressions, color schemes, and wording to match the media outlet’s tone and the client’s brand.
- Before the presentation, prepare anticipated questions and draft answers, or Q&A materials, and review them within the editorial team.
- Practice the speaking order, key points to emphasize, and timing of slide transitions in preparation for the presentation.
